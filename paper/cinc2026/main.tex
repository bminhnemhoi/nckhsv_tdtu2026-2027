\section*{\centering\normalsize Abstract}
\small\itshape
Multi-channel source separation is the usual answer to fetal QRS (fQRS) detection from abdominal
ECG; a one-lead device cannot use it. We ask how much of that benefit a small single-lead network
recovers, and measure it like-for-like: the published Power-MF pipeline is re-run by us under GNU
Octave on 22 women (ADFECGDB and the Silesian database, duplicates removed) and scored with the same
$\pm$50\ms\ matcher as our 113\,481-parameter dilated temporal convolutional network. Restricting
Power-MF to one lead costs it 12.12 F1 points (98.83 to 86.71); the network, on one label-blind lead,
scores 97.56 and recovers 10.85 of those points, 89.5\% (95\% CI 81.4--103.2), so it is not separable from
four-channel Power-MF ($-$1.27, CI {$[-3.08;+0.27]$}, $p=0.16$, better in 18 of 22 women, median
$+$0.23) and ahead of one-channel Power-MF ($+$10.85, 22 of 22). Across recorders the picture changes.
CinC 2013 set-a is not independent of ADFECGDB, as its organisers documented; we identify by
measurement the 15 records that are verbatim copies of our training data and score the other 60:
training on 22 women instead of five raises the label-blind score from 64.04 to 74.28
($p=3.5\cdot10^{-10}$), the oracle lead reaches 83.60, and the lead rule fails before the detector
does. A lead rule pre-specified as confirmatory gains $+$6.44 but misses Holm correction
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.051$); a post-hoc rule driven by the detector's own peak probabilities gains
$+$7.73 ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.0039$) and is reported as a hypothesis. At matched parameter count,
two receptive-field variants are equivalent to the network and a non-dilated CNN is worse only on the
logit scale; four unsupervised adaptation methods recover none of the cross-recorder gap.

\section{Introduction}
Fetal heart rate from abdominal electrodes needs a reliable fQRS detector, and a single-lead one
keeps the hardware small. The PhysioNet/CinC Challenge 2013 fixed the metric still in use, F1 with a
$\pm$50\ms\ window \cite{silva2013,clifford2014}; Behar et al.\ benchmarked template subtraction,
Kalman and blind-source methods under it \cite{behar2014}, and Andreotti et al.\ showed that
pre-processing changes the ranking of extraction algorithms \cite{andreotti2016}. Deep single-lead
detectors since \cite{zhong2018} include dual-path source separation \cite{shokouhmand2023} and
guided empirical mode decomposition \cite{xu2026}; the strongest recent multi-channel pipeline,
Power-MF \cite{jaeger2024}, combines two ICA stages with a matched filter and a power-spectral-density
(PSD) lead rule. Its numbers, like most, are quoted across papers rather than measured under one
matcher, and the lead is often chosen with the test labels.

The ingredients of our detector are established elsewhere and we claim none as new: per-sample
R-peak output \cite{zahid2022}, dilation as the useful architectural knob \cite{fotiadou2021}, the
10--60\Hz\ band \cite{xu2026}. What we contribute is measurement:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item the fraction of the multi-channel benefit one lead recovers, with the competitor executed by
  us on the same recordings and scored with the same matcher, per woman;
\item a cross-recorder evaluation on the 60 CinC 2013 set-a records that are not copies of the
  training data, separating detector loss from lead-rule loss, with the confirmatory and the post-hoc
  lead rule both reported;
\item matched-parameter architecture comparisons on the logit scale, and a negative result for
  four unsupervised domain-adaptation methods.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Data}
\textbf{ADFECGDB} \cite{goldberger2000}: five labour recordings (r01, r04, r07, r08, r10), four
abdominal leads at 1\,kHz, 5\,min each, fQRS annotated from a scalp electrode.
\textbf{Silesian database} \cite{matonia2020}: ten pregnancy recordings (B1, 32--42 weeks,
199.6\,min) and twelve labour recordings (B2, 5\,min), four abdominal leads at 500\,Hz, same hospital
and recorder as ADFECGDB. B1 annotations are indirect (author pipeline plus expert correction) and
in 5/10 records sit 8--12\ms\ after the abdominal R peak; B2 annotations come from a scalp electrode.
The overlap with ADFECGDB was noted by Baldazzi and Pani \cite{baldazzi2023}; cross-correlating all
$4\times4$ lead pairs we find that five B2 records \emph{are} ADFECGDB records (B2\_01\,=\,r01,
B2\_02\,=\,r10, B2\_07\,=\,r04, B2\_10\,=\,r07, B2\_11\,=\,r08; correlation 0.988--0.994). The
independent set is therefore 22 women (5 ADFECGDB, 10 B1, 7 new B2).

\textbf{CinC 2013 set-a} \cite{silva2013,clifford2014}: 75 records, four channels at 1\,kHz, 1\,min
each, a different recorder. Set-a is \emph{not} independent of ADFECGDB, and this was documented by
the Challenge organisers: 25 of the 447 Challenge records came from the ``Abdominal and Direct FECG''
collection (Table 1 of \cite{silva2013}, Table 2 of \cite{clifford2014}), and Clifford et al.\ cautioned
that an entry trained on it ``may have led to a bias in the results as this database was included in
set-a, set-b (and possibly a few records in set-c)'' \cite{clifford2014}. No accessible source states
\emph{which} set-a records they are. By normalised cross-correlation of the raw signals over all lags,
in two independent implementations, exactly 15 --- a03--a05, a08, a12--a15, a17, a19, a20, a22--a25 ---
are verbatim copies (NCC\,=\,1.0000 on all four channels, $|\Delta\mathrm{RR}|$\,=\,0.0\ms) of the
0--60, 120--180 and 240--300\,s windows of r01, r04, r07, r08 and r10; the other 60 reach at most 0.62.
Those 15 records score 99.87 under our 22-woman model and would have inflated 75-record means by
3.27--7.18 points. Every CinC figure below uses the 60 remaining records; a 53-record variant also
removes a33, a38, a47, a52, a54, a71 and a74, whose reference annotations are known to be unreliable
\cite{behar2014} (list fixed before the run).

\subsection{Detector}
One abdominal lead is band-passed 10--60\Hz\ (4th-order Butterworth, zero-phase) with a 50\Hz\ notch
and resampled to 250\,Hz. Maternal QRS are located on an 8--25\Hz\ envelope and removed by
subtracting a median beat template with per-beat least-squares scaling. The residual and the filtered
signal, each robust-scaled, form a two-channel 4\,s input. The network is a dilated residual TCN: a
stem and five residual blocks (dilations 1, 2, 4, 8, 16; 40 channels; kernel 7; BatchNorm; GELU) with
a $1\times1$ output convolution, 113\,481 parameters, receptive field 1\,516\ms. It emits one logit
per sample against a Gaussian heat-map target ($\sigma=12$\ms) at each annotated fQRS
\cite{zahid2022}, trained with weighted binary cross-entropy and AdamW (one-cycle). At inference,
overlapping windows are averaged and peaks above a threshold with a 250\ms\ refractory period are
the fQRS (0.48\,MB, 4.35\ms\ per window on a desktop CPU).

\subsection{Protocol and statistics}
Two models are used. The \emph{five-woman model} (m5) is leave-one-record-out (LORO) on ADFECGDB:
one test, one inner validation and three training records, six epochs; Silesian and CinC records
are scored zero-shot. The \emph{22-woman model} (m22) is trained on all 22 independent women with
11-fold grouped cross-validation (19 train, 1 validation, 2 test per fold, four epochs, on-the-fly
augmentation); CinC is scored zero-shot by the production checkpoint. In both, the threshold is chosen
on the validation subject from a grid and applied unchanged. Detections are matched one-to-one to
annotations by greedy nearest matching within $\pm$50\ms. Because a device sees one lead, the
headline lead is \emph{label-blind}: following Power-MF \cite{jaeger2024}, the lead whose residual
envelope has the largest Welch PSD peak in 1.8--3.0\Hz; the oracle lead (best F1 per record, uses
labels) is shown only as a ceiling.

The unit of analysis is the woman (the record for CinC), never the (record$\times$lead) pair. We
report mean differences with 95\% cluster-bootstrap percentile intervals (10\,000 resamples, seed 0)
and exact paired Wilcoxon tests; multiple lead rules are Holm-corrected over the family. ``No
difference'' claims use two one-sided tests (TOST) at a 1.0-point margin. Because F1 saturates near
100, architecture comparisons are repeated on the logit scale with a continuity correction of
$1/(4n_{\mathrm{ref}})$. With $n=5$ the smallest two-sided exact signed-rank $p$ is $0.0625$, so no
ADFECGDB comparison can reach $p<0.05$; we quote intervals there.

\subsection{Experiments}
\textbf{Power-MF} \cite{jaeger2024} was not quoted but executed: the authors' MATLAB pipeline was run
under GNU Octave 11.3 and scored with \emph{our} matcher, at four leads with both ICA stages (PMF-4)
and restricted to the single PSD-selected lead (PMF-1). Octave's \texttt{findpeaks} builds an
$O(k^{2})$ distance matrix for \texttt{MinPeakDistance} and overflows on long B1 recordings; we
re-implemented the MATLAB semantics in $O(k)$ (48/48 test cases). The port scores 99.40\,$\pm$\,0.51 on the
ten B1 records against 99.46 published by the authors. The \emph{recovery ratio} is
$(\mathrm{ours}-\mathrm{PMF1})/(\mathrm{PMF4}-\mathrm{PMF1})$, bootstrapped over women.

\textbf{Lead rules.} Seven label-blind alternatives to the PSD rule were fixed in a written plan
before any was evaluated on CinC, together with the decision rule ``confirmatory rule = the one with the
highest in-domain F1 over 22 women''. The plan was written after per-lead F1 had been computed and is
not anchored by a third party, so we call it a written plan and nothing stronger. Rules read only
the detector's output and classical signal-quality indices (SQIs): \emph{gate} (a reject gate trained
on PSD-lead segments; keep the lead with the highest $1-\bar{P}(\mathrm{bad})$), \emph{gate4} (same,
trained on all four leads), \emph{rrcv} (lowest RR-interval coefficient of variation), \emph{peakprob}
(highest mean detector probability at its own detected peaks; no training, no hyper-parameter),
\emph{rrplaus}, a learned selector and a four-lead fusion.

\textbf{Architecture.} Six alternatives to the TCN at matched parameter count ($\pm$2.7\%) on the
22 women: a wide (3\,052\ms) and a narrow (748\ms) receptive field, a three-branch multi-scale TCN, a
non-dilated CNN widened to the same 1\,508\ms\ field, a 1-D U-Net and a small-field CNN (60\ms).
Three folds, three epochs, one seed, so the run is a scaled-down replicate of the production protocol.
The band was also varied on the TCN (10--60 vs 1--45\Hz, four epochs).

\textbf{Domain adaptation}, hyper-parameters chosen on a 12-woman-to-B1 transfer and never on CinC:
an adaptive mains notch (50 and/or 60\Hz, whichever the record's spectrum shows), confidence-thresholded
self-training, AdaBN \cite{li2018} and TENT \cite{wang2021}.

\textbf{Reject gate.} A 4\,s segment is \emph{bad} if the detector's F1 on it is below 80. A
gradient-boosted classifier on 12 classical SQIs \cite{andreotti2017} is trained
leave-one-\emph{subject}-out over the 22 women (3\,890 segments, 5.40\% bad) and scored both pooled and
within record; intervals resample subjects.

\subsection{How much of the multi-channel benefit one lead recovers}
Table~\ref{tab:pmf} is the like-for-like comparison. Restricting Power-MF to one lead costs it 12.12
F1 points (98.83 to 86.71, CI $[-18.27;-6.90]$, worse in 21 of 22 women): that is the measured price
of the four-channel ICA stages on this material. Our single-lead network scores 97.56 and recovers
10.85 of those points ($+$10.85 over PMF-1, CI $[+6.80;+15.40]$, $p=4.8\cdot10^{-7}$, 22 of 22 women),
a recovery ratio of 89.5\% (cluster-bootstrap CI 81.4--103.2; leave-one-woman-out range 88.6--93.4).
Against the full four-channel pipeline it is not separable ($-$1.27, CI $[-3.08;+0.27]$, $p=0.16$); on
ADFECGDB the interval excludes zero in its favour ($+$0.39, CI $[+0.22;+0.60]$, 5/5).

The mean deficit is not uniform: the median difference over 22 women is $+$0.23 and the network is
better in 18. Three recordings --- B1\_07 ($-$12.88), B1\_06 ($-$10.51), B2\_03 ($-$9.77) --- pull the
mean below zero. The defensible statement is that one lead recovers most of the multi-channel
benefit, not that it matches four leads on every woman.

\subsection{CinC 2013: the 60 records that are not training data}
Under the label-blind PSD rule, training on 22 women instead of five raises the mean from 64.04 to
74.28 ($+$10.24, CI $[7.13;13.60]$, $p=3.5\cdot10^{-10}$, better in 52 records, worse in 3); records at
or above 90 go from 27 to 33 and those below 50 from 22 to 16; the 53-record variant gives 64.19 to
75.27 ($+$11.08, $p=4.4\cdot10^{-9}$). The median record of m22 is 95.07: the distribution is split,
not shifted. One-lead Power-MF on the same 60 records scores 55.97 (Table~\ref{tab:pmf}).

The lead rule loses accuracy across recorders before the detector does: the PSD rule is 9.32 points
below the oracle lead (74.28 vs 83.60, CI $[5.10;14.15]$) and picks it in only 17 of 60 records.
Table~\ref{tab:cinc} shows four of the seven pre-specified rules; the three omitted (rrplaus, learned, fuse) are the weakest and are in the released JSON. The written decision rule selected
\emph{gate} as confirmatory; it gains $+$6.44 over PSD (CI $[+2.49;+11.10]$, $p=0.010$) but
\emph{does not survive} Holm correction over the seven rules ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.051$). We report
this as a failed confirmatory test. Within the same family, \emph{gate4} (81.01, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.015$) does survive: the family-level test succeeds while the single designated rule does not. The in-domain arbiter was also uninformative by construction: the
four best rules lie within 0.04 points of each other on 22 women, where the whole oracle headroom is
1.08 points, a design error on our part. The strongest of the seven is \emph{peakprob}: 82.01 against
74.28, $+$7.73 (CI $[+3.82;+12.41]$, $p=5.6\cdot10^{-4}$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.0039$ over the family),
82.9\% of the oracle headroom, records below 50 from 16 to 9, 19 wins / 35 ties / 6 losses, no record
losing more than 3.90 points. \emph{peakprob was chosen after the CinC results were seen}; the reason
to still report it is that the effect survives Holm correction over the whole pre-specified family. On
the logit scale over the 22 in-domain women the ranking changes --- \emph{gate} leads under two of
three record pairings and \emph{peakprob} is third ($+$0.13 logit vs PSD, CI $[-0.04;+0.41]$) --- so
the post-hoc problem does not disappear. No public dataset with real fQRS labels exists on which to
replicate it (NIFEADB, NInFEA, nifecgdb and set-b ship none), so it remains a strong, unconfirmed
hypothesis.

\subsection{Architecture at matched parameters, on the logit scale}
Table~\ref{tab:arch} compares six alternatives to the TCN on the 22 women. Two are \emph{equivalent}
under TOST after Holm correction: the wide receptive field (3\,052\ms, $-$0.01 F1, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}$
$7\cdot10^{-10}$) and the multi-scale TCN ($-$0.02, $4.6\cdot10^{-6}$); beyond 1.5\,s more context
buys nothing. Two are worse on both scales: the 1-D U-Net ($-$1.25, logit $-$0.80 $[-1.17;-0.43]$)
and the 60\ms\ CNN ($-$3.10, worse in 20 of 22, logit $-$1.64). The instructive case is the non-dilated
CNN widened to the same 1\,508\ms\ field: on the F1 scale it ties ($-$0.80, CI $[-2.40;+0.13]$,
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.30$), on the logit scale it is worse ($-$0.45, CI $[-0.75;-0.16]$), and the
narrow field (748\ms) sits between ($-$0.71; logit $-$0.29 $[-0.68;+0.12]$). F1 near 100 hides
differences that the logit scale resolves, so every architecture claim here is stated on both.
Changing the band on the TCN from 1--45 to 10--60\Hz\ gives $+$2.44 on the PSD lead (CI
$[-0.04;6.29]$; three women carry all of it) and $-$0.07 $[-0.51;0.39]$ averaged over four leads: the
band is not an established effect on this network.

\subsection{Domain adaptation, reject gate, clinical read-out}
On the 60 CinC records none of the four unsupervised methods improves the PSD-lead score of 74.28:
adaptive notch $+$0.25 (CI $[-0.25;+1.08]$, $p=0.70$), self-training $-$0.67 $[-1.11;-0.29]$, AdaBN
$-$1.58 $[-2.50;-0.73]$, TENT $-$2.43 $[-3.64;-1.35]$. The three measured shifts between the datasets
(60\,s duration, 10.13-bit quantisation, 60\Hz\ mains at 1.64 times the noise floor), imposed on the
in-domain recordings, cost 0.01 points. Whether the remaining gap is fetal signal
absent from the lead or signal the model misses could not be decided: the visibility test built for
that purpose has an 18.0\% false-negative rate (CI 12.1--25.0) on these records.

The LOSO reject gate reaches a pooled AUROC of 0.965 (CI $[0.857;0.992]$) and a within-record AUROC of
0.934 (CI $[0.872;0.981]$, over the 11 women with both classes). Ranked by gate score, the three women
that carry the deficit against four-channel Power-MF (B2\_03, B1\_07, B1\_06) are the three least
reliable of 22. Short-term variability computed from detected beats is biased $+$0.33\ms\ on ADFECGDB
(limits of agreement $[-0.89;+1.55]$); on an earlier ten-record CinC subsample the bias was
$+$20.50\ms, so the detector is usable for trending on gated records and not as a standalone STV
monitor.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{One lead recovers most of what four leads buy.} Multi-channel separation is worth a
directly measured 12.12 F1 points to Power-MF itself; a 113\,k single-lead network recovers 10.85
of them. The residual gap is concentrated in three of 22 recordings, and the reject gate ranks
exactly those three as least reliable, which is what a one-lead device must do: fail loudly, not
often.

\textbf{Across recorders the lead rule fails before the detector.} Training on 22 women recovers 10.24 points on
CinC without closing the recorder gap; the PSD rule is within 1.90 points of the oracle in domain and
9.32 below it out of domain. A rule that lets the detector score the leads is the obvious repair, but
the confirmatory version of that idea missed Holm correction and the version that passed was chosen
after the data were seen; the next held-out set must be opened with the rule fixed beforehand.
Four adaptation methods that repair the shifts we can measure recover nothing, and the shifts
themselves cost 0.01 points in domain; we do not conclude from this that the signal is missing,
because the test that would show it has an 18\% false-negative rate.

\textbf{Architecture is a small variable, and F1 is the wrong scale to see it.} At matched
parameters two variants are equivalent to the TCN and one (dilation at equal field) differs only on
the logit scale. Nothing here is claimed as new; the receptive field near 1.5\,s and dilation
are the two design choices we can resolve with 22 women.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) $n$: five women on ADFECGDB and 22 in all, one hospital, one recorder;
every interval is wide and no ADFECGDB comparison can reach $p<0.05$ at subject level. (ii) Against
four-channel Power-MF the point estimate is $-$1.27; the interval contains zero, but the sign is
against us. Power-MF ran under Octave at our resampled rate with our matcher, not in the authors'
evaluation. (iii) The lead-rule plan was written after per-lead F1 existed and is not third-party
anchored; \emph{peakprob} is post hoc; no third labelled dataset exists to confirm it. (iv) The
architecture and band runs use three folds, three to four epochs and one seed; the main model one seed
(a second seed moved the mean by 0.03). (v) B1 labels are indirect, so B1 F1 measures agreement with
the authors' pipeline. (vi) Whether the 60 clean CinC records come from 60 distinct women was not
checked; NCC $\le$ 0.62 excludes verbatim copies only. (vii) The reject gate has not been re-scored
on the 60 clean CinC records; gate figures computed earlier on the contaminated 75-record set are withdrawn with the rest.

\section{Conclusion}
Measured under one matcher on 22 women, four-channel Power-MF gains 12.12 F1 points over its own
single-lead restriction and a 113\,k single-lead network recovers 10.85 of them (89.5\%): not separable
from four channels ($-$1.27, CI $[-3.08;+0.27]$), ahead of one channel in 22 of 22 women. On the 60
CinC 2013 set-a records that are not copies of the training data the label-blind score is 74.28 after
training on 22 women, 9.32 below the oracle lead: cross-recorder lead selection, not the detector, is
the open problem, and the best candidate rule is a hypothesis awaiting a labelled third dataset.
